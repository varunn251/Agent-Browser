# Guest-post prospecting workflow

## 1. Define the target

Confirm or infer only from explicit user context:
- Target niche and closely related topics.
- Target country, language, and audience.
- Desired opportunity type: editorial guest article, expert contribution, columnist program, resource contribution, or paid placement.
- Exclusions such as competitor sites, marketplaces, agencies, or paid opportunities.
- Requested prospect count and required fields.

Keep distinct opportunity types separate. A site that sells a backlink or link insertion is not automatically an editorial guest-post opportunity.

## 2. Discover candidates

Search with several query families rather than relying on one search:
- `[topic] "write for us"`
- `[topic] "guest post guidelines"`
- `[topic] "contributor guidelines"`
- `[topic] "become a contributor"`
- `[topic] "submit an article"`
- `[topic] intitle:contributors`
- `[topic] "editorial guidelines"`

Add target country, city, audience, or subtopic terms when they matter. Search results are leads, not proof. Open each candidate's own site and verify it there.

## 3. Verify the opportunity on the site

Look for:
- A current contributor, guest author, submission, or editorial-guidelines page.
- Evidence that the site publishes articles from external contributors.
- Recent articles and a coherent topical focus.
- Named editors, authors, or an identifiable publisher/business.
- Clear editorial, disclosure, and correction practices.
- A working contact or submission route.

Record the exact evidence URL and a short factual note. If a page is missing, say "not found" or "not verified"; do not infer acceptance from a search snippet.

## 4. Quality review

Assess these dimensions independently. Use a 0–5 score for each and explain the evidence.

| Dimension | What to assess |
| --- | --- |
| Topical relevance | How closely the site's real content and audience match the target niche |
| Editorial authenticity | Original, useful articles; identifiable authors/editors; meaningful editorial standards |
| Site and audience fit | Coherent purpose, active publishing, useful reader experience, appropriate geography |
| Transparency | Publisher identity, contact details, contributor disclosures, sponsorship clarity |
| Opportunity clarity | Current guidelines, submission route, eligibility, and transparent costs |

A score is a prioritization aid, not a search-engine metric or guarantee. Keep risk flags separate from the score. Disqualify or strongly downgrade sites that mainly exist to sell links, have unrelated topic clusters, publish thin/spun content at scale, or conceal their commercial model. A single weak signal is not proof of a private blog network or manipulation; describe the evidence and mark uncertain cases for manual review.

Do not invent Domain Authority, Domain Rating, organic traffic, keyword counts, or backlink metrics. Only report them when a user-provided source or an authorized measurement tool supplies the value, and record its source and date.

## 5. Classify the opportunity

Use one of:
- **Editorial — verified:** current first-party guidelines or a clear, active editorial contribution route was observed.
- **Editorial — likely:** strong evidence of external contributors, but current submission rules are unclear.
- **Paid placement:** the publisher explicitly offers sponsored/paid posts or link placements. Record the stated price and terms only when visible; do not present it as unpaid editorial.
- **Not accepting / closed:** the site explicitly says submissions are closed or unavailable.
- **Unverified:** insufficient first-party evidence.
- **Reject:** irrelevant, deceptive, clearly link-selling, or otherwise unsuitable, with a reason.

## 6. Contacts and outreach handoff

Prefer the first-party contact or submission route. Record where the contact information was published. Do not guess email patterns or substitute an address from another source when the user's source spreadsheet is authoritative. If the user has restricted recipient emails to an approved spreadsheet, use only those exact email addresses and flag missing or ambiguous matches.

Research does not authorize outreach. Do not submit pitches, send email, or create accounts unless the user explicitly asks and approves the intended content and recipients.

## 7. Suggested output fields

Use the guest-post CSV template. Include at least:
- Website/domain and niche.
- Opportunity classification.
- Guidelines/submission URL and contact page URL.
- Evidence note and checked date.
- Score by dimension, total score, and rationale.
- Paid/free/unknown status.
- Duplicate status, risk flags, and next action.
