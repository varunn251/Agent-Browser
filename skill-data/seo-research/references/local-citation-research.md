# Local citation research workflow

## 1. Establish the business source of truth

Before looking for listings, obtain the canonical business details from the user or an approved business data source:
- Exact business name.
- Physical address or approved service-area details.
- Main phone number, including country code.
- Website URL.
- Primary and secondary categories.
- Target cities/regions and country.
- Any existing profile URLs or listing exports.

Do not invent a location, use a virtual address as a physical storefront, add unsupported service areas, or alter the business name to insert keywords. Mark missing information as unknown and ask for it when it blocks a reliable comparison.

## 2. Discover relevant citation sources

Search in separate categories:
- **Core business platforms:** major map, navigation, and business-profile platforms relevant to the country.
- **Local sources:** chambers of commerce, local business associations, municipal or regional business resources, and reputable local directories.
- **Industry sources:** professional associations, trade groups, licensed-provider directories where relevant, and niche directories.
- **General directories:** only when they have a genuine audience, clear ownership, usable moderation, and geographic coverage.

Use search queries such as:
- `[city] business directory`
- `[city] chamber of commerce member directory`
- `[industry] directory [country]`
- `[industry] association member directory [region]`
- `[city] [industry] businesses directory`
- `[business name] [city]` to look for existing listings.

Treat search results as candidate sources. Verify listing eligibility and requirements on the directory's own website.

## 3. Verify each directory

Record:
- Directory name and canonical domain.
- Country, region, or industry served.
- Whether a business listing is actually available.
- Eligibility requirements and whether the business appears eligible.
- Submission method: self-service, claim-existing, email, or editorial review.
- Price and renewal terms when explicitly displayed.
- Verification steps and required NAP fields.
- Link or profile fields offered, if visible.
- Evidence URL and date checked.

Classify the source as **free**, **paid**, **freemium**, **unknown**, or **not accepting listings**. Do not assume a listing is free because a signup page is visible. Do not describe a directory as authoritative or high quality without evidence.

## 4. Check existing listings and duplicates

Search the business name with its city, phone, and domain. Review likely matches individually.

Compare:
- Normalized business name.
- Address components (street, locality, city, region, postal code, country).
- Phone number.
- Website domain.
- Existing profile/listing URL.

Normalize for comparison only: lowercase text, normalize whitespace and punctuation, standardize phone formatting when the country is known, and canonicalize URLs. Preserve the original displayed NAP in the report. Do not merge two businesses solely because they share a name or building.

Use these statuses:
- **Exact duplicate:** reliable matching evidence across multiple identifiers.
- **Likely duplicate:** strong but incomplete match; manual review required.
- **Different business:** meaningful identifiers differ.
- **Not found:** searches were performed but no matching record was observed.
- **Unclear:** evidence is insufficient or contradictory.

Never claim that every duplicate has been found. Search coverage is incomplete and directories may hide or de-index records.

## 5. Ethical listing workflow

Research first and prepare a submission checklist. Do not:
- Create a listing for a location the business does not occupy or serve.
- Add fake reviews, fake photos, fabricated credentials, or inaccurate categories.
- Create multiple listings for one eligible location.
- Claim or edit a business profile without the user's approval.
- Submit forms, upload documents, pay fees, or accept contractual terms without explicit approval.
- Bypass CAPTCHAs, login walls, or directory access controls.

For duplicate profiles, recommend the directory's documented merge, correction, or support process rather than creating another profile.

## 6. Suggested output fields

Use the local-citation CSV template. Include directory category, geography, eligibility, cost, submission method, evidence, checked date, listing-match status, NAP discrepancies, and a clear next action.
